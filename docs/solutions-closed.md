# Commercial Solutions for Floor Plan Extraction

*Research catalogue · October 2026 · Commercial products, APIs, on-premise software and services that turn existing plans (raster, PDF, DWG) into structured floor data, or that cover parts of the chain (CAD validation, area measurement, CAFM import with ERP integration), rated for BBL's use case. Open-source tools and models are in [open-source solutions](solutions-open.md); the open-source tables still at the end of this document will move there. Training data is in [open datasets](datasets-open.md) and [closed datasets](datasets-closed.md), needs and constraints in [motivation and goals](motivation-goals.md). Bracketed numbers refer to the shared [source list](papers/sources.md).*

## 1. Scope and Method

**Scope.** Commercial offers that BBL could buy or test today, in eight groups:
- plan conversion products and APIs that read raster plans, PDFs or DWGs and return rooms, walls and openings (§3.1);
- raster-to-vector software that runs on-premise (§3.2);
- construction takeoff tools that detect rooms and areas on plans (§3.3);
- indoor mapping platforms that import CAD or plan images (§3.4);
- CAD validation, area measurement and CAD toolkits (§3.5);
- CAFM/IWMS and ERP add-ons that import plans and link rooms to the ERP (§3.6);
- digitising, area measurement and scan-to-plan services, mainly in Switzerland (§3.7);
- labelling and model platforms that support BBL's own pipeline (§3.8).

The evidence on accuracy is summarised in §3.9. Research methods without released code are in the [literature review and state of the art](literature-review.md); open-source tools and models are out of scope here.

**Method.** Desk research in October 2026: vendor product pages, documentation, privacy and data-processing pages, plus news and independent reviews where they exist, and BBL's own market screening\[1\]. No vendor was contacted, no account was registered and no quote was requested, so hosting details and contract terms remain open. Prices are not recorded: they change often and are available from the vendors.

**Table layout.** All tables in §3 use the same columns:
- *Task*: what the product extracts or does; *Inputs*: accepted plan formats.
- *Deployment and hosting*: SaaS, API, desktop, on-premise or service; data location as published.
- *Data rights*: who owns the output and whether the vendor may use uploaded plans, as stated in terms or DPAs; "not found" where the terms were read without result, "not checked" where they were not read.
- *ERP / CAFM*: published integrations with ERP real-estate modules or CAFM systems.
- *Accuracy evidence*: "claim" marks vendor statements; "independent" marks third-party tests; "BBL test" marks BBL's own tests.
- *Relevance* for BBL: High = fits the hosting and ERP constraints and could be tested or procured now; Medium = useful for part of the chain, for non-sensitive plans, or once hosting is clarified; Low = wrong input or output, cloud-only outside Switzerland without an alternative, no longer available, or would duplicate the ERP-based area management.

## 2. Key Findings

1. **No product covers the chain end to end.** None found reads room stamps (AOID, usage, area) into ERP fields, derives the gross floor area per floor and runs in Switzerland. The market splits into cloud converters, takeoff tools (areas only), indoor mapping platforms (wayfinding models), on-premise vectorisers (lines only), ERP/CAFM front-ends (need clean CAD) and manual services (§3.1–§3.7).
2. **No AI converter offers Swiss hosting.** EU hosting exists (Archilogic, Mappedin, AmpliFY), Swiss hosting does not. Four on-premise routes with room recognition were found, each with a caveat: AxcelerateAI builds custom pipelines on the client's servers; RasterScan ships a Docker image; Archilyse's AGPL pipeline survives in forks; CSoft's PlanTracer forms rooms on AutoCAD, with current availability unverified (§3.1, §3.2).
3. **Data rights differ.** Archilogic owns the models it generates from customer plans, CubiCasa owns its deliverables, and Bimify's terms allow training on deliverables despite "you own your data" on its website. AxcelerateAI (client owns code and weights), AmpliFY (training only on opt-in) and BIMxAI leave ownership with the customer. Any test, even on non-sensitive plans, should be preceded by a check of these terms (§3.1).
4. **All accuracy figures are vendor claims.** The published figures use undisclosed test sets and are not comparable. The only independent result is BBL's own test of one converter on a public historical building, which did not read doors or room stamps; the vendor states that it supports residential plans only (§3.9).
5. **Room areas on PDFs are commercially mature; room stamps are not.** Takeoff tools (Togal, Kreo, STACK) detect enclosed rooms and their net and gross areas automatically, and indoor mapping platforms (Mappedin, Pointr) output rooms, doors and levels as GeoJSON or IMDF. None documents reading AOIDs or usage from room stamps, BBL's core CAFM requirement (§3.3, §3.4).
6. **The ERP end of the chain has a product category.** ERP real-estate modules offer graphical-integration interfaces for CAD partners that link closed room polylines in CAD or IFC to the ERP's room objects, with the drawing supplying the areas. This is the natural landing step for extraction output after validation, without a second area-management system. DACH CAFM suites handle CAD and DIN 277 areas well, but each would be a second system, and none mentions SIA 416 (§3.6).
7. **The ERP vendor's real-estate roadmap matters.** Any plan front-end should write to the ERP through documented interfaces and be checked for a migration path (§3.6).
8. **A Swiss public precedent exists for the manual route.** Kanton Zürich awarded a "data trustee" contract after an open tender: area data for many floors captured or migrated from mixed sources and formats, CAD plans edited and migrated into the ERP, plus building-services data and maintenance. It is the closest template for BBL's worst scans (§3.7).
9. **On-premise vectorisers solve a smaller problem.** VPstudio (softelec, Munich) and WiseImage (CSoft) run locally and turn scans into clean DWG lines and text, but not rooms; room formation would remain BBL's pipeline (§3.2).
10. **Microsoft's internal "AI Maps" pipeline confirms BBL's vector route.** It parses CAD with open-source libraries, normalises inconsistent names with an LLM and writes GeoJSON for more than 500 buildings; it is not sold (§3.4).

### 3.6 CAFM/IWMS and ERP Integration

BBL wants plan data to land in the ERP's real-estate module, not in a second area-management system. Two kinds of products matter:
- **Graphical front-ends inside the ERP** that link CAD room polygons to the ERP's room objects through the vendor's CAD-provider interface.\[322\] In this model the drawing supplies the measurements, and the room ID in the drawing (the AOID) links each polygon to its object in the ERP.\[323\]
- **CAFM suites with their own CAD module and an ERP interface.** They import DWG, compute areas to DIN 277 and push data to the ERP, but each is an area-management system in its own right.

The ERP vendor's real-estate roadmap matters for both.\[324\] A plan front-end bought now should write to the ERP through documented interfaces and be checked for a migration path.

### 3.9 Accuracy Evidence

No vendor publishes independently verified accuracy figures. What exists:
- **Vendor benchmarks and claims.** Several vendors publish accuracy or deviation figures\[297\]\[269\]\[273\]\[251\]. None states its test set, so they are not comparable.
- **Case studies.** Vendor case studies report time savings against manual modelling\[92\]\[260\]. None is independent and none gives an accuracy figure.
- **BBL's own test** of one converter (§3.1) is the only independent result: doors and room stamps were not read.
- **A public precedent for manual services:** Kanton Zürich's data-trustee contract (§3.7) shows the scope of the manual route, though not its accuracy.

## 4. Recommended Next Steps

1. **Test on non-sensitive plans only**, such as the public historical plans in the pilot's benchmark set, with the same harness and correction-minutes metric as the in-house pipeline ([pipeline §8](pipeline.md#8-evaluation)). Check each vendor's terms first (§2, finding 3).
   - Mappedin: the only self-service converter for raster, PDF and DWG; compare its rooms and doors with the pilot.
   - Kreo or Togal: automatic room areas on PDFs, as a reference for area error.
   - AxcelerateAI: a one-plan analysis, then a fixed-scope proof of concept only if the result is strong.
   - RasterScan: run the Docker image locally; no data leaves BBL.
   - WiseBIM and Bimify (once Instant or the API is live): IFC route for comparison.
2. **Ask in writing about Swiss hosting or on-premise** (a request for information under procurement law, not a quote):
   - Archilogic: whether the Enterprise "private instance" can run in Switzerland, and who owns the generated models.
   - Mappedin and Pointr: Swiss or on-premise deployment, and export of rooms with external IDs (AOIDs).
   - AxcelerateAI: on-premise build in Switzerland, IP transfer, and training-data rights.
   - Archilyse pipeline: terms and support for a commercial on-premise licence.
   - NavVis: self-hosted IVION or Swiss data residency for scanning projects.
3. **Clarify the ERP landing step with BBL's ERP team:** which graphical integration is in place today, whether a partner product using the ERP's CAD interface can run in BBL's environment and handle SIA 416 area types, and how the vendor's roadmap affects it.
4. **Prepare a service tender for the worst scans**, modelled on Kanton Zürich's data-trustee contract (§3.7), with the providers in §3.7 as candidates. Require:
   - processing and drafting in Switzerland;
   - delivery to BBL's CAD guideline, checked automatically before acceptance;
   - the original scan and the compliant DWG delivered as pairs, with rights to use them as training and evaluation data.
5. **Use services as a label source.** The paired output from step 4 is labelled, in-domain data for the hardest styles. A self-hosted CVAT or Label Studio covers corrections and extra labels without sending plans out.
6. **Where no usable plan exists, buy on-site capture**: laser scanning with local processing and plans delivered to BBL's CAD guideline (providers in §3.7).

## 5. Limitations of This Catalogue

- Desk research only. No vendor was contacted, one product was tested on a public plan (BBL test), and no quote was requested, so hosting details and contract terms remain open.
- Accuracy figures are vendor claims unless marked otherwise; they are not comparable across rows.
- Not covered: Alpha Takeoff, Cubit, German-language AI takeoff tools, French start-ups beyond WiseBIM, LandingAI and Azure/Google document AI, ClearEdge3D, FARO, Autodesk ReCap, a list of Swiss NavVis partners, and DACH CAFM tools such as VI-Space, dataBAT, Facilitycloud, IMS, mobilis and the SIA 416 area tools of ArchiCAD and Vectorworks.
- Several vendor pages blocked automated access or returned errors. Facts taken only from search snippets are marked as such.
- Data-rights and AI-training clauses were found for some vendors only; "not found" does not mean the vendor makes no use of the data.
- Prices are not recorded; they change often and are available from the vendors.
- Not legal or procurement advice. Hosting, data protection and the Information Security Act need BBL's own assessment before any plan leaves BBL.
